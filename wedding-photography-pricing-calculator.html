How Much Should You Charge for Wedding Photography in 2026?

Pricing your wedding photography correctly is one of the most important business decisions you will make. Charge too little and you attract clients who undervalue your craft, burn out faster, and struggle to grow. Charge too much too early and you price yourself out of the bookings you need to build your portfolio. This calculator is designed to help you find the right starting point based on real market data.

How Market Location Affects Your Wedding Photography Rates

Your geographic market is one of the strongest pricing factors. A photographer in rural Arkansas and a photographer in Manhattan may have identical skills, but their markets support vastly different rates. High-cost markets like New York City, Los Angeles, San Francisco, London, and Dubai typically command rates 1.5x to 2x higher than mid-market cities. Low-cost markets (rural areas, small cities) typically see rates 25–35% below mid-market averages.

Our calculator applies a market multiplier of ×0.7 for low-cost markets, ×1.0 for mid-markets, and ×1.6 for high-cost cities. These multipliers are based on aggregated pricing data from wedding photography communities, industry surveys, and published rate guides for 2026.

Why Most Wedding Photographers Undercharge

The biggest pricing mistake in wedding photography is not overcharging — it is undercharging. Most photographers at the 1–3 year mark are delivering work that justifies mid-tier pricing ($3,000–$5,000) but are still charging beginner rates ($800–$1,500). This creates a cycle where you attract price-sensitive clients, work harder for less money, and never build the brand equity needed to raise your rates.

If you are consistently booked 3–6 months in advance, that is the clearest signal that your pricing is too low. Being fully booked is not a badge of honour — it means you are leaving money on the table and working at unsustainable volume.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much should I charge for wedding photography in 2026?

Wedding photography pricing in 2026 depends on your experience, market, and wedding type. Beginners (0–1 year) typically charge $600–$1,500 for standard weddings in mid-markets. Established photographers (3–5 years) charge $3,500–$7,500. Luxury photographers (8+ years) in high-cost markets like NYC or London can charge $16,000–$32,000+ per wedding.

What is the average wedding photographer cost in 2026?

The average wedding photographer cost in 2026 ranges from $2,500 to $5,000 in the US and £1,500 to £3,500 in the UK for a mid-level professional shooting a standard wedding with 8 hours of coverage. These figures increase significantly in high-cost cities like New York, Los Angeles, and London.

How do I price my wedding photography packages?

Price your wedding photography packages using a structured approach: start with a base rate anchored to your experience level, adjust for your geographic market tier, factor in wedding type, set pricing for your coverage hours, and add percentage-based pricing for extras like second shooters, albums, and drone coverage.

How much should a beginner wedding photographer charge?

A beginner wedding photographer (0–1 years) should charge between $600 and $1,500 for a standard wedding in a mid-market area. The most common mistake beginners make is pricing below $500, which attracts budget clients who undervalue the work and slows portfolio growth.

Should I charge per hour or per wedding?

Most professional wedding photographers charge per wedding (package pricing) rather than per hour. Package pricing accounts for the full scope of work including pre-wedding consultations, travel, shooting, culling, editing, and delivery. Hourly rates undervalue the 30–60+ hours of total work behind each wedding.

How much more should I charge for destination weddings?

Destination wedding photography typically commands 2x to 3x the rate of a standard local wedding. If you charge $3,500 for a standard wedding, your destination rate should be $6,000–$12,000 before travel expenses. This accounts for additional planning, travel days, and logistical complexity.

When should I raise my wedding photography prices?

Raise your prices when you are consistently booked out 3–6 months in advance, when inquiry quality is consistently high, after every 5–8 weddings in your first two years, or when your portfolio clearly outperforms your current pricing tier. Most photographers should aim to increase rates by 20–30% annually in their first five years.

How much should I charge for a second shooter?

A second shooter typically adds 15–20% to your wedding photography package price. If your base package is $3,500, adding a second shooter would bring it to $4,025–$4,200. Some photographers charge a flat fee of $500–$1,500 depending on their market.

What is the difference between UK and US wedding photography pricing?

UK wedding photography pricing tends to run 15–25% lower than US pricing for equivalent experience levels. A mid-level UK photographer charges £1,500–£3,500 while a comparable US photographer charges $3,000–$6,000. High-cost city pricing in London is roughly equivalent to New York.
