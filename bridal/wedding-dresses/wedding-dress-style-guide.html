The Complete Wedding Dress Style Guide

From silhouettes and necklines to fabrics and body-type matching — everything you need to find the wedding dress that feels unmistakably you.

Explore Silhouettes

Silhouettes

Every Wedding Dress Silhouette, Explained

The silhouette is the foundation of your entire bridal look. Understanding how each shape fits, flows, and flatters will help you walk into your first appointment with confidence — and know exactly what to ask for.

01

Most Popular

A-Line

Fitted at the bodice and gradually flaring from the natural waist, creating a universally flattering triangular silhouette. Works beautifully for nearly every body type and wedding setting.

Best for: All body types

02

Dramatic

Ballgown

Features a fitted bodice with a dramatic, full skirt that begins at the natural waist. Think fairy-tale romance — perfect for grand venues, cathedrals, and formal celebrations.

Best for: Pear, hourglass, petite

03

Body-Conscious

Mermaid

Hugs the body closely from bust to knee, then flares dramatically at or below the knee. A show-stopping shape that highlights curves and creates a stunning entrance.

Best for: Hourglass, athletic

04

Elegant

Trumpet

Similar to mermaid but flares at mid-thigh rather than the knee, offering a gentler, more comfortable curve. Beautifully balances drama with ease of movement.

Best for: Hourglass, tall, athletic

05

Modern

Sheath

Follows the body’s natural line from shoulder to hem with minimal flare. Effortlessly chic for modern brides, intimate weddings, city venues, and beach ceremonies.

Best for: Petite, straight, tall

06

Versatile

Fit & Flare

Sits between A-line and mermaid — fitted through the bodice and hips, flaring above the knee. Offers curve definition without the tightness of a mermaid, with beautiful movement on the dance floor.

Best for: Hourglass, pear, athletic

Necklines

A Guide to Wedding Dress Necklines

Your neckline frames your face, shoulders, and décolletage. The right choice can elongate your neck, balance your proportions, and set the tone for your entire bridal look.

01

Romantic

Sweetheart

A curved, heart-shaped neckline that dips in the centre, beautifully highlighting the décolletage. One of the most popular and romantic neckline choices for brides.

Flatters: Most bust sizes

02

Elongating

V-Neck

Creates an elongating vertical line that draws the eye down, making the torso appear longer and slimmer. Pair with a pendant necklace or let the neckline speak for itself.

Flatters: Fuller bust, round faces

03

Classic

Off-the-Shoulder

Sleeves sit below the shoulders, elegantly framing the collarbone and shoulders. A timeless choice that adds romance and softness to any silhouette, from A-line to ballgown.

Flatters: Broad shoulders, narrow hips

04

Timeless

Strapless

A clean, strapless edge that showcases the shoulders, arms, and décolletage. Versatile enough for nearly any silhouette and pairs beautifully with statement earrings or a veil.

Flatters: Toned arms, full bust

05

Delicate

Illusion

Sheer fabric (typically tulle or mesh) with lace or beading over the décolletage, creating the illusion of a higher or lower neckline. Offers coverage with elegance and intricate detailing.

Flatters: All body types

06

Regal

Bateau (Boat Neck)

A wide, horizontal cut that follows the collarbone from shoulder to shoulder. Exudes understated elegance and is famously associated with royal wedding fashion — think Meghan Markle and Audrey Hepburn.

Flatters: Narrow shoulders, small bust

Fabrics

Wedding Dress Fabrics & Textiles

Fabric determines how your dress moves, drapes, and photographs. Understanding textiles helps you choose a gown that suits both your silhouette and your season.

01

Luxurious

Satin

A smooth, luminous fabric with a subtle sheen that catches light beautifully. Heavier weight gives structure to ballgowns and A-lines. Best for autumn and winter weddings.

Season: Autumn & Winter

02

Dreamy

Tulle

A lightweight net fabric that creates volume and an ethereal, romantic look. Often layered for ballgowns and A-lines. Soft tulle drapes gently, while stiffer tulle adds dramatic volume.

Season: All year

03

Romantic

Lace

Intricate open-weave fabric with floral or geometric patterns. Varieties include Chantilly (delicate), Alençon (corded), and guipure (bold). Adds texture, romance, and vintage character.

Season: Spring & Summer

04

Flowing

Chiffon

A lightweight, sheer fabric with a beautiful flowing drape. Ideal for beach and garden weddings, layered skirts, and creating effortless movement. Feels cool and comfortable in warm weather.

Season: Spring & Summer

05

Understated

Crepe

A matte fabric with subtle texture that drapes smoothly and photographs beautifully. Perfect for modern, minimalist gowns. Lightweight crepe works year-round and resists wrinkles.

Season: All year

06

Structured

Mikado

A structured silk blend with a gentle lustre and smooth finish. Holds its shape beautifully, making it ideal for clean, architectural gowns with dramatic bows, structured bodices, or sculptural trains.

Season: Autumn & Winter

Body Types

Finding Your Dress by Body Type

Every body is a bridal body. These recommendations are starting points, not rules — the most important thing is how you feel. But understanding which shapes tend to flatter your proportions can make the process more enjoyable and focused.

Hourglass

Balanced bust and hips with a defined, narrower waist. Your natural proportions are beautifully symmetrical.

  • Mermaid or fit-and-flare to celebrate your curves
  • Belted A-line to define the waist
  • Sweetheart or V-neck necklines
  • Avoid boxy shapes that hide your waist

Pear

Hips wider than shoulders and bust, often with a beautifully defined waist. The goal is to balance the upper and lower body.

  • A-line or ballgown skirts to balance hips
  • Embellished or detailed bodice to draw the eye up
  • Off-the-shoulder or bateau necklines
  • Avoid clingy fabrics below the waist

Apple

Fuller midsection with slimmer legs and often a generous bust. Focus on creating a defined waistline and elongating the torso.

  • Empire waist to create a high, defined waistline
  • A-line or flowing skirts that skim the midsection
  • V-neck necklines to elongate the torso
  • Avoid tight-fitting mermaid styles

Athletic & Straight

Shoulders, waist, and hips roughly the same width with a toned, athletic build. The goal is to create the illusion of curves and definition.

  • Mermaid or trumpet to create curves
  • Peplum or structured bodice for waist definition
  • Sweetheart or strapless necklines
  • Ruching and textured fabrics add dimension

Petite

Under 5’4” (163 cm) in height. The key is to elongate the body and avoid overwhelming your frame with too much volume or fabric.

  • Sheath or column dresses to elongate
  • A-line with a defined high waist
  • V-neck to create vertical lines
  • Avoid overly voluminous ballgowns

Curvy & Plus Size

Full, beautiful curves throughout the body. Focus on highlighting your best features and finding a dress with proper support and structure.

  • A-line with a structured bodice for support
  • V-neck or sweetheart necklines to elongate
  • Fitted bodice with a flowing skirt
  • Look for built-in corsetry and boning

Expert Advice

Shopping & Fitting Tips

Walking into a bridal boutique for the first time can feel overwhelming. These practical tips from bridal stylists will help you make the most of every appointment.

01

Start Early

Begin your search 9–12 months before the wedding. Designer gowns take 4–6 months to order, plus 2–3 months for alterations. Early shopping gives you the most options.

02

Book Smart

Schedule appointments midweek when boutiques are quieter. Bring no more than two trusted people — too many opinions can cloud your instincts and create unnecessary pressure.

03

Try Three Silhouettes

Even if you have a clear vision, try at least three different silhouettes. Many brides are surprised to find that their favourite dress is nothing like what they originally imagined.

04

Wear the Right Underwear

Wear nude or seamless underwear and a strapless bra. Bring shoes close to the heel height you plan to wear. Most salons provide clips and accessories for trying on.

05

Trust the Process

Sample sizes rarely match your exact measurements — focus on the silhouette, neckline, and fabric rather than the fit. Alterations work wonders. Let the stylist clip and pin to show the potential.

06

Set a Budget (and Stick to It)

Be upfront about your budget with your stylist. Include alterations (typically £200–£600), accessories, and undergarments in your total budget. Beautiful dresses exist at every price point.

FAQ

Common Questions About Wedding Dresses

The six main wedding dress silhouettes are A-line, ballgown, mermaid, trumpet, sheath, and fit-and-flare. A-line is the most universally flattering, fitted at the bodice and gradually flaring from the waist. Ballgowns feature a fitted bodice with a dramatic full skirt. Mermaid dresses hug the body and flare at or below the knee. Trumpet gowns are similar but flare at mid-thigh. Sheath dresses follow the body’s natural line, and fit-and-flare sits between A-line and mermaid.

For pear-shaped bodies, A-line and ballgown silhouettes balance wider hips. Hourglass figures look stunning in mermaid or fit-and-flare styles that accentuate natural curves. Petite brides should consider sheath or A-line dresses with a defined waist to elongate the frame. Athletic builds suit styles with ruching, embellishments, or structured bodices that create the illusion of curves. Apple-shaped bodies benefit from empire waists and flowing A-line skirts.

The most flattering neckline depends on your body and personal style. V-neck and sweetheart necklines suit most bust sizes and elongate the neck. Off-the-shoulder necklines highlight the collarbone and shoulders. Illusion necklines offer coverage while still showcasing details. Bateau (boat) necklines create an elegant horizontal line that broadens narrow shoulders. Strapless styles work well with a fuller bust and toned arms.

For summer weddings, lightweight and breathable fabrics are ideal. Chiffon is a beautiful choice for its flowing drape and airy feel. Organza offers a slightly stiffer structure while remaining lightweight. Crepe is smooth, comfortable, and photographs beautifully in warm light. For outdoor summer ceremonies, avoid heavy satins and instead consider lace overlays on lightweight linings for a romantic yet comfortable look.

You should start shopping for your wedding dress 9 to 12 months before your wedding date. Most designer gowns take 4 to 6 months to order and produce, and you’ll need an additional 2 to 3 months for alterations. If you’re purchasing off-the-rack or from a sample sale, 3 to 6 months may be enough. Allow extra time for destination weddings or custom designs.

Wear nude or seamless underwear and a strapless bra (or go braless if comfortable, as many gowns have built-in support). Bring shoes close to the heel height you plan to wear on the day. Avoid heavy makeup that might transfer onto white fabric. Wear a button-down top or wrap dress for easy changes. Most bridal salons provide clips and undergarments for trying on gowns.
